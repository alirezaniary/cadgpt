The brand mark's ground

The mark is two-tone and both tones are figure: a navy bracket pair with a blue crosshair through it. It was drawn for a light ground. This product has none — --bg and --card sit within a hair of the mark's own navy — so half the logo is not low-contrast, it is missing.

Every panel shows the same surfaces at their real sizes: the topbar over the report, so the brand is judged against the PASS/FAIL/INDETERMINATE colors it has to share a screen with; the sign-in card at its real width; and a 16px favicon in a light and a dark tab strip, because the browser's ground is the one we do not own. Compare down the page, not across a swatch.

measured 2026-09-12

WCAG 2.1 asks 3:1 for a graphical object that carries meaning. Today both of the mark's tones are under it on both app surfaces — the blue is not surviving, it is failing more slowly.

Unrelated to the mark, found while measuringon --cardwas recorded as
--accent #3165ea — the "ساخت حساب کاربری" link, .table a:hover, the active workspace row2.48:13.84:1 (the old orange)
--accent-fg on --accent — every button label4.61:15.21:1 (the old orange)

This is a separate defect and is not fixed here. When the accent was swapped to the logo's blue so the mark would not read as a mismatched import, the app's link color fell from 3.84:1 to 2.48:1 and docs/design/DESIGN.md was never updated — its contrast table still records the orange. Treatment 1 removes the reason for that swap: once the mark sits on its own ground it no longer needs the UI to match it. Same hue and saturation at #4776EC reaches 3:1 and at #7699F1 reaches 4.5:1. Which of those, if any, is a product decision, not one to take inside a logo task.
